# D02: stock put/call open-interest ratio (contrarian and confirmatory): diagnosis

Train split (2015-01-01 to 2020-12-31), week horizon, `top` selection unless noted. Replays:
`experiments/done/d02-stock-pcr-contrarian-week/` and `.../d02-stock-pcr-confirmatory-week/`. Card:
`cards/D02.yml`. Joint test: `reports/evidence_train.md`.

## What the card tests

Per stock, PCR = near-month put OI / call OI (summed across strikes), and an "extreme" is `|z| >= 2`
against the stock's own trailing 20 sessions. The catalog left the direction open, so it was ported as
two strategies that share one core and differ only in sign:

- **Contrarian:** extreme-high PCR (heavy put positioning) is bullish, extreme-low PCR is bearish.
- **Confirmatory:** the opposite.

So contrarian-bearish and confirmatory-bullish pick the same stocks (extreme-low PCR) with opposite
calls, and likewise for the other pair. The two replays are one experiment read both ways.

The two halves mirror each other as they should (+3.75 vs -3.99 on the same stocks), which is a useful
consistency check on the scoring. The information is all on one side: **stocks whose put/call ratio has
collapsed to an extreme low (calls crowded relative to puts) tend to lag the market the following week.**
Extreme-high PCR carries nothing in either direction.

Contrarian-bearish per-year edge: -1.38, -2.14, +5.95, +3.52, +10.22, +5.52 (2015-2020): negative in the
first two years, then positive four years running, with 2019 carrying the most. The `all` selection agrees
(+3.44, t 1.59, directional move +0.26%). Fewer than five picks a date on average (661 over 225 dates,
about 2.9), because extremes are rare, so the table would often be part-empty.

Joint test (week/bearish, 23 experiments, SPA p=0.190, StepM empty): contrarian-bearish ranks **first** at
+3.75, interval -0.64 to +8.36, P(edge<=0)=0.050. That is still short of individual significance after
snooping, but it is the strongest week-bearish reading in the lab.
