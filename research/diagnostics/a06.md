# A06: low volatility (Ang, Hodrick, Xing and Zhang 2006): diagnosis

Train split (2015-01-01 to 2020-12-31), week horizon, `top` selection (5 picks/table/date) unless noted.
Source replay: `experiments/done/a06-low-volatility-week/`. Joint test: `reports/evidence_train.md`.

## What the card tests

The paper finds high idiosyncratic-volatility US stocks earn low returns over the next month, and low
ones earn high returns, measured cross-sectionally on factor-model residuals. `cards/A06.yml` tests a
simpler, per-symbol version at a one-week horizon: `score = 1 - sigma_recent / sigma_baseline`, the
stock's last 20 sessions of total (not residual) volatility against its own prior 60. So bullish here
means "calmer than this stock's own recent normal", not "a low-volatility stock". That is a
time-series volatility-change signal, a real departure from the paper (flagged on the card).

## Does not qualify as a go-live P1 candidate

Bullish has the positive edge but loses after costs (-0.50%). Bearish has a positive but negligible
edge (+0.15, t 0.12) and a negative directional move (-0.02%). Neither side clears SPEC.md section 10's
provisional rule.

## Overlap

A05B's bullish picks (least lottery-like, cross-sectional) share about 46% of A06's bullish top-5 each
date (approximate, before the universe filter). Both reward a quiet recent month, measured two ways.
For a consensus strategy they count as one family, not two.

## What to try next

  sigma, lowest bullish), as a new card, so "low-volatility stock" and "calmer than its own normal" are

- The 2018-2019 dip lines up with weak market-line years. If the regime filter in P4 is built, this
  card is a good place to test it, since its failure years are concentrated.
