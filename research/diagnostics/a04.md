# A04: 52-week-high proximity (George and Hwang 2004) — diagnosis

Train split (2015-01-01 to 2020-12-31), week horizon, `top` selection (5 picks/table/date) unless noted.
Source replay: `experiments/done/a04-52-week-high-week/`. Joint test: `reports/evidence_train.md`
(week/bullish, week/bearish cells).

## What the card tests

George and Hwang (2004) find a US stock's nearness to its own 52-week high predicts future returns
better than its past return alone — stocks near their high keep outperforming, stocks far below it keep
underperforming, over a published horizon of several months to a year. `cards/A04.yml` tests whether any
of that continuation survives at NSE's one-week horizon, far shorter than the paper's own claim (flagged
as a red flag on the card, same transfer-horizon stress test as A03's). Score is the stock's own close
centred within its trailing 252-day high/low range: `2 * (close - low) / (high - low) - 1`, so a close at
the 52-week high scores +1 (most bullish) and a close at the 52-week low scores -1 (most bearish) — a
per-symbol adaptation of the paper's cross-sectional decile sort, flagged on the card as a deliberate
change to what "farthest from the high" means.

Bullish is the better side but still inside noise (t=1.28). Per-year edge: -1.67, +0.58, +3.95, +0.98,
+5.67 (2016-2020) — mostly positive with a loose upward drift across the 5 covered years, but on only 5
points that is a pattern to flag, not to trust. Bearish is flatter and sign-unstable: +1.43, -2.64, +1.09,
-2.24, -0.13, with only 2 of 5 years positive — reads as noise around a slightly negative mean, not a
usable bearish signal. Both sides lose money after costs (bullish -0.55%, bearish -0.34%): the pick-weighted
move is negative even where the hit-rate edge is positive, same shape as A03's result.

The `all`-selection numbers (every non-abstaining pick, ~18-20k per side, since this card never abstains)
are smaller but tighter: bullish +0.92 (t 1.63), bearish +0.84 (t 1.97), both with 80% positive years and
both still losing on directional move after costs (-0.55%, -0.19%). The `all` picks don't change the
conclusion — same sign, same after-cost loss — just average out some of `top`'s date-to-date noise.

## Best of a weak cohort, not a real finding

The joint test puts this in context: on week/bullish (21 experiments, SPA p=0.769 — the single weakest
cell of all four horizon/side combinations) A04 actually ranks **1st**, with P(edge<=0)=0.088, the
closest to the StepM 10% bar of anyone in that cell — but StepM still names zero strategies individually
superior, and the cell-wide SPA says nothing in it beats the market line once snooping is accounted for.
Being the best of a field that collectively clears nothing is not evidence of an edge; it is evidence
that the field is flat. On week/bearish (23 experiments, SPA p=0.190, StepM empty) A04 sits in the lower
third at -0.50, unremarkable in either direction.

## Does not qualify as a go-live P1 candidate

SPEC.md section 10's provisional rule needs ≥500 train picks (A04 clears this on both sides), a positive
train edge (bullish only — bearish is negative) and a positive train directional move for that side.
Bullish has the positive edge but fails the directional-move leg (-0.55%); bearish fails both legs. Not a
candidate on either side.

## A04 vs A03: two different "stale information" tests, same negative result

A03 (12-1 month momentum, skip the most recent month) and A04 (52-week range position, which already
embeds a similar lookback) both test a published multi-month continuation effect at a one-week horizon,
and both come back null after costs despite one side nominally clearing a positive-but-insignificant
edge (A03's bearish +0.85 t=0.61; A04's bullish +1.92 t=1.28). Unlike A01's week-bearish reversal, which
clears all three provisional bars on a *very recent* (trailing-week) signal, neither of these *stale*
multi-month signals shows a usable one-week edge — this is the second card in a row saying published
long-horizon anomalies do not telescope down to NSE's one-week table, not a single unlucky card.

## What to try next

- Not worth tuning `window_days` on train: the bullish signal's own joint-test standing (best of a cohort
  that clears nothing) means a grid search here is more likely to find noise than a real plateau.
- If this family is revisited, test it at something closer to its own published horizon (a multi-week or
  monthly rebalance) rather than one week, same caveat A03's diagnosis already raised for 12-1 momentum.
- No confluence variant is proposed: like A03, there is no positive-and-profitable base edge on either
  side for a filter to sharpen.
