# O01 (order-block retest) vs O00 (placebo) vs the market line: train diagnosis

This is the first of the two comparisons `docs/orderblock_hypotheses.md` says settle "do order blocks
help?" — the other is O02 vs D01 alone (`research/diagnostics/o02.md`). Its own stated bar: **"A strategy
helps only if O01 beats O00 and the market line."** Sources: `experiments/done/o0{0,1}-{placebo,retest}-{day,week}/results.md`.

## Reading it against the lab's own bar

**O01 does not clear "beats O00 and the market line" on any of the four horizon/side cells.**

- **Day bearish** and **week bearish** are the strongest case for the impulse requirement: both beat O00
  by a similar margin (+1.31 and +1.66 points), and both land at a small positive or flat edge rather than
  O00's clearly negative one. But neither is statistically distinguishable from zero (|t| < 1 on both), so
  "beats the market line" is not established — it's "no longer loses to it, within noise."
- **Day bullish** also beats O00, but stays net-negative (-0.47) — the impulse requirement helps a little,
  it just doesn't help enough to turn a loser into a winner.
- **Week bullish is the one cell where the impulse requirement actively hurts**: O01 is *worse* than the
  placebo by 1.6 points (-2.21 vs -0.61), and is the single worst number in this whole table. Requiring a
  genuine >=1.5 ATR impulse that breaks the 10-bar high, on a week horizon, for bullish zones specifically,
  removes picks that were (mildly) useful rather than picks that were noise.

## Where it works and fails

- **By side:** the impulse requirement consistently helps the bearish side (both horizons) and is mixed-to-
  harmful on the bullish side. This is the opposite of a clean story — if order blocks were a real
  structural effect you'd expect the impulse filter to help symmetrically on both sides, not flip sign by
  side.
- **By year (day horizon, bearish — O01's best cell):** 2018 is the standout (+3.86 edge, accuracy 57.43%);
  2015 and 2017 are mildly positive (+1.35, +0.19); 2016, 2019, 2020 are flat-to-negative. 3 of 6 years
  positive (50%), same as O00's own day-bearish split, so O01 isn't converting more years to positive, just
  shifting the magnitude in the already-positive and already-negative years differently.
- **By year (week horizon, bullish — O01's worst cell):** only 2015 is positive (+1.40); every other year
  is negative, 2016 and 2020 sharply so (-5.36, -4.84). This cell also has O01's lowest coverage among the
  four (99.34%, still near-complete, so this isn't an abstention artefact).
- **Coverage:** O01 abstains more than O00 on every cell except week-bullish, as expected — the impulse
  and structure-break requirement is strictly narrower than O00's "any last opposite candle." Day bearish
  drops from 100% to 97.41% coverage, day bullish from 99.86% to 99.51%, week bearish from 100% to 97.34%.
  None of this is large enough to explain the sign flips above by itself.
- **Sector / weekday breakdown:** not available from this run. `picks.json` carries only `{date, symbol,
  score}`, not outcomes, and the engine sandbox never holds the joined dataset needed to compute a
  weekday- or sector-level hit rate (`SPEC.md` "Data" section — the full dataset lives only in the Actions
  cache). If this card survives to a validation query, worth asking a supervised or Actions-side run to
  emit a sector/weekday cut directly from `engine/experiment_report.py` rather than trying to reconstruct
  it from picks alone.

## Answer to "do order blocks help?" (train only, so far)

**Not yet, and the honest train-split answer leans no rather than "not measured."** O01 beats the placebo
O00 on 3 of 4 cells, consistent with the impulse/structure-break requirement filtering out *some* genuine
noise relative to "any last opposite candle" — but it clears the market line on **zero** of 4 cells, and on
the fourth cell (week bullish) the impulse requirement makes things measurably worse than the placebo. None
of the differences reach significance in either direction. This is consistent with order blocks, as this
lab has operationalised the public screenshots, carrying no real train-split edge — at best a bearish-only,
non-significant hint that the strict impulse definition filters slightly better than a naive one, which is
a much weaker claim than "order blocks work." O02 (next: `o02.md`) asks whether adding the OI confirmation
the vendor also displays changes this picture — bearish does improve further there, bullish gets worse.
