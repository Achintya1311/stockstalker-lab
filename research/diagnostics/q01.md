`experiments/done/q01-price-volume-corr-week/`. Card: `cards/Q01.yml`. Rule: rho = 20-session correlation of the
close with log volume; a rise on thinning volume (r20 > 0, rho < 0) is called bearish, a fall on thinning volume
(r20 < 0, rho > 0) bullish; volume that grows with the move abstains.

## Where it works and fails

- **The divergence reading is backwards on NSE F&O stocks.** A move on thinning volume does not fade: a fall on
  thin volume keeps falling over the next week (week bullish -3.39, every year between -2.5 and -4.4), and a
  rise on thin volume keeps rising the next day (day bearish -1.51). The textbook "no volume, no conviction"
  story predicts the opposite. The likelier mechanism is a slow, low-participation drift that persists, which
  matches the mild whole-universe persistence the lab keeps finding (T02, I01, I02, T23).
- **The week-bullish top five are an independent set.** Approximate top-5 overlap (no universe filter; the
  dataset is not in this sandbox): 3.2% with A01 week-bullish, 5.5% with T19 week-bullish, 5.5% with Q05
  week-bearish, 2.3% with I01 week-bearish, 1.4% with T05C, 1.0% with D02-contrarian. So it is not a relabelled
  momentum or reversal card.
- **Mirror arithmetic for a flip** (as T05C was predicted): the week-bullish picks' gross move is -0.79% + 0.415%
  (delivery cost) = -0.37%. Called bearish instead, they would earn about +0.37% - 0.25% (futures cost) =
  **+0.12% after costs**, with an edge near +3.4 on the bearish market line. That would clear the provisional
  letter for week-bearish and sit next to A01's released +4.73. The day cells have no usable flip: day bullish
  flipped is about -0.06% after costs, day bearish flipped pays the delivery cost on a gross move of -0.10%.
