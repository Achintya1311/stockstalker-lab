## Where it works and fails

- **The score is a state, not a crossing.** `strategies/t02_golden_death_cross.py` scores the spread
  `(SMA50 - SMA200) / SMA200` every week, so it reads "which side of the 200-day trend is this stock on", and
  the top five are the most stretched stocks on each side.
- **A small, steady trend-persistence effect in the whole universe**, about one point on both sides
  (t 2.6 and 3.1), but it does not get stronger at the extremes: the top five are no better than the average
  and far noisier. This is the opposite of the short-horizon results (T05, T08, T09, champion v1), where the
  most extended stocks lag. A slow trend state persists mildly; a fresh burst reverses.
- **It does not pay its costs.** The bullish move before costs is about -0.12% a week, so a higher hit rate
  comes with smaller wins than losses. The bearish move before costs is about +0.04%, far short of the
  0.25% futures cost.
- **Little overlap with the lab's week-bearish choice:** 12.5% of T02's bearish top five are in A01's, and
  20.9% in T01's 20/50 bearish top five (T01 is +3.20 there, also not eligible on cost).

## What to try next

- Nothing as a stand-alone strategy. The `all`-selection persistence is a candidate **filter**, not a signal:
  for example, keep a week-bearish pick only when its 50-day SMA is below its 200-day. That would be a new
  pre-declared card on top of A01 or T05C; low priority before go-live.
