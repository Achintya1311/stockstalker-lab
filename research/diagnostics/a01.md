# A01 (short-term weekly reversal, Lehmann 1990 / Jegadeesh 1990): train diagnosis

`cards/A01.yml`: rank NSE F&O stocks by trailing 5-trading-day return. Biggest losers score bullish for
the coming week, biggest winners score bearish. `score = -(trailing return)`, continuous and signed.
Week horizon only — the card's own `horizon` field, and the oldest reversal paper on the catalog (pre-
1990, so idea vintage is not the open question; NSE market structure is). Source:
`experiments/done/a01-weekly-reversal-week/results.{json,md}`, `reports/evidence_train.md`'s week/bullish
and week/bearish tables (block-bootstrap + SPA/StepM/MCS).

## The headline (top / 5-pick selection, train 2015-2020)

| Side | Edge (pts) | t | 95% bootstrap interval | Positive years | Coverage | Dir. move after costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bullish | -0.03 | -0.02 | -2.23 to +2.03 | 3 of 6 (50%) | 100% | -0.51% |

No leak alarm on either side (53.19% long-sample bearish accuracy, well under the 70%/15pt trigger).

## Reading it

**Same asymmetry shape as the day-horizon fade family (A02, A08, A09), now on a week horizon and the
opposite leg of the trade.** Fading last week's winners to bearish beats the market line by 2.48 points
with t=1.83 — the univariate bootstrap interval is -0.09 to +5.31, which technically still touches zero,
but the one-sided read is strong (`P(edge<=0)=0.031`). It's not nothing. Betting on last week's losers to
bounce bullish is flat: -0.03 points, t=-0.02, an interval centred almost exactly on zero, and only 3 of 6
years positive — no bounce effect survives in this universe over this period.

**It does not survive multiple-testing correction.** `reports/evidence_train.md`'s week/bearish table
covers 23 experiments; its SPA p-value is 0.190 (joint null — "no strategy here beats the market line" —
is not rejected) and **StepM names zero strategies individually superior** for week/bearish, the only
side/horizon cell in the whole evidence report where StepM's list is empty. A01-bearish sits 4th of 23 by
raw edge, ahead of several cards that do clear SPA/StepM elsewhere, which says the week/bearish cohort as
a whole is thin, not that A01 is special within it. The univariate P(edge<=0)=0.031 is a real number but
a much weaker claim once 23 trials are priced in; take this as "not yet shown," not "confirmed," pending
more train years or validation data narrowing the interval.

**Year pattern is less consistent than A02's bearish side, more like A08's.** Bearish: 2015 +2.15, 2016
+5.91, 2017 +2.36, 2018 **-1.55**, 2019 +1.99, 2020 +4.10 — one losing year (2018), no clear trend, not
monotonically decaying the way A08-bearish's six straight positive-but-shrinking years were. Bullish:
2015 +0.95, 2016 -0.74, 2017 -2.50, 2018 -1.71, 2019 +0.98, 2020 +2.98 — no pattern at all, three losing
years scattered through the middle of the split, consistent with "no effect" rather than "effect
decaying" or "effect regime-dependent."

**Directional move after costs is the detail worth flagging.** Bearish's after-cost directional move is
+0.02% — on the right side of zero, in the same razor-thin-but-positive band as A09-bearish's +0.03%
(the only other card in the lab with a clearly-positive hit-rate edge whose directional move doesn't flip
negative after costs). A02-bearish and A08-bearish both have hit-rate edge but a *negative* directional
move (-0.04%, -0.04%) — "the hit rate works, the money doesn't." A01-bearish and A09-bearish are the two
exceptions so far, and they sit on different horizons (week vs day) and different mechanisms (reversal on
last week's return vs gap-fade), so this isn't the same signal counted twice.

**Sector / weekday breakdown: not available from this run** — same limitation flagged in
`a02.md`/`a08.md`/`a09.md`/`o01.md`-`o05.md`: `picks.json` carries only `{date, symbol, score}`, and this
sandbox never holds the joined dataset to cut by sector or weekday.
