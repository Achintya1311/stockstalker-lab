# A05: MAX effect / lottery stocks (Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw 2011) — diagnosis

Train split (2015-01-01 to 2020-12-31), week horizon, `top` selection (5 picks/table/date) unless noted.
Source replay: `experiments/done/a05-max-effect-week/`. Joint test: `reports/evidence_train.md`
(week/bearish cell; A05 has no week/bullish row, see below).

## What the card tests

Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw (2011) find that US stocks with an outsized best day over the past month
(high MAX, "lottery-like") underperform over the following month, and low-MAX stocks outperform.
`cards/A05.yml` tests whether that shows at NSE's one-week horizon, far shorter than the paper's own
holding period (red flag on the card, same transfer-horizon stress test as A03/A04). Score is
`-z`, where `z = MAX / sigma`: the largest daily return over the trailing 21 sessions, divided by the
standard deviation of daily returns over the 60 sessions before that window.

## The bullish side was never tested: the score is not centred

The replay's `top / bullish` and `all / bullish` sections both read "No in-universe picks". This is a
construction defect, not a result. The card and module docstring say `z` is "centred" against the
stock's own volatility, but dividing by sigma only rescales MAX; it does not move its centre. MAX is the
largest of 21 daily returns, so it is almost always positive, which makes `score = -z` almost always
negative. Checked directly from `picks.json`:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Scored symbol-dates | 61,270 |
| Positive (bullish) scores | 12 (0.02%), none of them in the F&O universe on their date |
| `z` percentiles 1 / 5 / 25 / 50 / 75 / 95 / 99 | 0.47 / 0.83 / 1.38 / **1.92** / 2.67 / 4.68 / 7.75 |

The median `z` of 1.92 is close to the expected maximum of 21 independent standard-normal draws (about
1.89), which is what an uncentred MAX/sigma should look like. A score of zero would need every one of
the month's 21 daily returns to be zero or negative.

The unit test `test_a_muted_best_day_relative_to_baseline_scores_bullish` passed because its fixture
makes every day in the month fall 2%. That is the only way to get a negative MAX, and it does not happen
on real data, so the test proved the sign arithmetic but not that the bullish side can fire. This is the
same class of gap the lab has flagged before: a synthetic fixture that reaches a branch real data never
reaches.

## Numbers (bearish side only)

The bearish table is a valid test of the paper's main claim: the five most negative scores each date are
the five highest `z`, i.e. the most lottery-like months relative to the stock's own volatility.

| Side | Picks | Coverage | Accuracy | Market line | Edge | t | Positive years | Dir. move after costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bearish (highest MAX/sigma) | 1,475 | 100% | 50.37% | 50.65% | -0.28 | -0.22 | 3/6 (50%) | -0.37% |
| Bullish (lowest MAX/sigma) | 0 | 0% | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a | n/a |

Per-year bearish edge: -4.34, -1.78, -2.64, +0.71, +1.22, +3.72 (2015-2020). That is a steady upward drift
from clearly negative to positive, but on six points with t=-0.22 overall it is a pattern to note, not a
signal. The `all` selection (41,979 bearish picks, every symbol, since nearly every score is negative)
has edge -0.00 (t -0.14) and is meaningless as a bearish test: it is "every stock is bearish", so its
accuracy is just the market line.

Joint test: in the week/bearish cell (23 experiments, SPA p=0.190, StepM empty) A05 sits mid-table at
-0.28 (interval -2.73 to +2.12, P(edge<=0)=0.572). Unremarkable either way.

## Does not qualify as a go-live P1 candidate

SPEC.md section 10's provisional rule needs at least 500 train picks, a positive train edge and a positive
train directional move on a table. Bearish fails both the edge and the directional-move legs. Bullish has
no picks.

## A05 among the anomaly cards so far

Third published multi-week or multi-month cross-sectional anomaly in a row (after A03 momentum and A04
52-week high) with no usable one-week signal on the side that was actually tested. A01 (trailing-week
reversal) is still the only A-series week card with a positive-and-profitable side.

## What to try next

- **Port a new version, A05 v2, before drawing any conclusion about the bullish side.** Versions are
  immutable, so this is a new strategy, not an edit. Two centring options, in order of preference:
  1. Cross-sectional, as the paper does: on each date, rank MAX/sigma across the F&O universe and score
     `-(pct_rank - 0.5)`, so the bottom half is bullish and the top half bearish. This makes the score
     depend on which peers are present (same caveat `cards/O04.yml` carries), so the card should say so
     and use O04's narrower causality audit.
  2. Per-symbol, keeping this card's own design: subtract the expected maximum of `MAX_WINDOW`
     standard-normal draws (about 1.89 for 21) from `z` before negating it. This keeps the score
     independent of peers, but it assumes normal returns, and NSE daily returns have fat tails, so
     the true centre will sit a little higher.
  Either way, the bullish unit test should use a realistic month: mixed up and down days whose best day
  is small relative to sigma, not a month where every day falls.
- Re-read the bearish side after v2 lands, since a cross-sectional rank may reshuffle which five stocks
  each date counts as most lottery-like.
- Not worth tuning `max_window` or `sigma_window` on v1: half the card was never tested and the half that
  was is a clean null.
