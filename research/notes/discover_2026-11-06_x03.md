# Discover batch, 6 Nov 2026: X03 101 Formulaic Alphas, fourth batch (F13 to F17)

Five more rows from Kakushadze (2016), "101 Formulaic Alphas", Wilmott 84, [arXiv:1601.00991](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.00991).
Formulas were checked against the paper's appendix A.1 on 6 Nov before the rows were written; the catalog text is the
lab's own summary, no text or code is copied. All five need only ohlcv (no VWAP). Chosen because each conditions a
short-horizon reading on something F01-F12 did not: price acceleration with a volume surge, a 100-session trend regime,
the length of an up or down streak, the co-movement of range position and trading, and the recency of the 10-session
high.

| Row | Alpha | Horizon | Why it is worth a trial |
|---|---|---|---|
| F13 | #17 | day | F11's range-top fade (+4.47 day bearish) plus two extra conditions: the last move accelerated and turnover surged. Asks whether F11's edge concentrates on accelerating, heavy-volume range tops. |
| F14 | #24 | day | A regime switch: in a flat or falling 100-session trend, fade the distance above the 100-session low; in a rising one, fade the 3-session move. No lab card switches the reading on the trend. |
| F15 | #30 | week | Counts up and down closes over the last three sessions and weights by the 5/20-session volume ratio: a streak reversal, the week table's A01 seen through direction counts instead of size. |
| F16 | #55 | day | Minus the 6-session correlation between the rank of the close's place in its 12-session range and the rank of turnover: buying into range highs (positive correlation) is bearish. Q02 correlates returns with volume; this correlates position. |
| F17 | #60 | day | Volume-weighted close location of the last bar, against how recently the 10-session high was set. A close near the bar's high on heavy trading, with no fresh 10-session high, is bearish. |

Data notes: the paper's `volume` and `adv20` are replaced by traded value (`turnover`) where they are compared across
stocks or with each other (F13, F15, F16, F17), as in F09, so share-count changes after splits do not move the ratios.
Price-unit terms are turned into returns by dividing by the close (F13's second difference uses log closes; F14's
distance above the low and 3-session change are divided by the close). Paper products that are one-signed are centred
by the date's median (F13, F15, F17), and the card says so. `ts_argmax` (F17) is read as the position of the maximum
within the window, 1 for the oldest to 10 for the last session.

Red flags for every row: published 2016 (train 2015-2020 is mostly pre-publication, so decay cannot be seen on train);
WorldQuant used them as small weights in a large book, not stand-alone signals; the paper gives no hit rate.

Not added: #7 (a volume-gated 7-session move, close to F09, which was a null), #10 (a consistency-gated one-day move,
as #9/F03), #33 (the last bar's own return, as F01/F05), #8 and #49/#51 (price-unit thresholds that do not carry across
price levels), #40/#44 (high-volume correlations, as Q02), the VWAP alphas (#83 to #99; no VWAP in the bhavcopy).

Port order: F13 (extends the best F reading), F16, F17, F14 (day), then F15 (week).
