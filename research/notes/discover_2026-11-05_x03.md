# Discover batch, 5 Nov 2026 (IST): X03 101 Formulaic Alphas, third batch (F08 to F12)

Five more rows from Kakushadze (2016), "101 Formulaic Alphas", Wilmott 84, [arXiv:1601.00991](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.00991).
Formulas were checked against the paper's appendix A.1 on 5 Nov before the rows were written; the catalog text is the
lab's own summary, no text or code is copied. All five need only ohlcv and no VWAP. Chosen because each asks something
F01-F07 did not, and because the H batch closed with only H02 eligible, so the next trials go back to the short-horizon
reversal family where the lab's train edge lives (day bearish), plus two week readings for the weaker week tables.

| Row | Alpha | Why it is worth a trial |
|---|---|---|
| F08 | #19 | A01 fades last week's raw move; this fades the 7-session move and weights it by the yearly return rank. Asks whether the week reversal is stronger for last year's winners (week bearish) or losers (week bullish). |
| F09 | #43 | The only bullish-first reversal in the batch: a 7-session fall met by unusually heavy trading. Day bullish is the lab's weakest table; T04/Q06 are the readers there and neither uses volume. |
| F10 | #34 | Pairs a one-day reversal with a volatility-compression ratio; nothing in the lab conditions the fade on recent calm versus turbulence. |
| F11 | #38 | Fades closes near a 10-session high on an up bar: a cheap check of whether F07's fade on highs carries over to closes inside a range. |
| F12 | #52 | A pullback low inside a strong 11-month trend on heavy volume, bullish at the week horizon: momentum entered on weakness, the opposite of how A03/H05 enter. |

Data notes: the paper's `adv20` is dollar volume while `volume` is shares; F09 uses traded value for both (ratio of
today's turnover to its 20-session mean). F12's first factor is in price units in the paper; the port divides it by
the close so stocks of different price are comparable. F09's paper form is one-signed (a product of two ranks in
0..1); the port centres it by the date's median so it has a bearish side, and says so in its card.

Red flags for every row: published 2016 (train 2015-2020 is mostly pre-publication, so decay cannot be seen on train);
WorldQuant used them as small weights in a large book, not stand-alone signals; the paper gives no hit rate.

Not added: Alpha#4 (time-series rank of a cross-sectional rank of the low price level, which barely moves for price
levels), #20 (needs the pick day's open, as T24), #54 (a rescaled close location, as Q06/F01/F05), #13/#16 (price-volume
covariance, as Q02).

Port order: F09 (day bullish), then F08 (week), then F10, F11, F12.
